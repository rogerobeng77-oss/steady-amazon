# Where Steady's pictures come from

Every picture in this app can be traced to a file page that states its licence, and the
table below was generated from what Wikimedia Commons actually returned rather than from
a claim on a landing page. `tools/fetch_photos.py` rejects anything whose licence forbids
commercial use or derivative works before it is ever downloaded.

## Two registers, doing two different jobs

**The hero band is a photograph of real people.** It runs the full width of the top of
every screen and its job is to say who this is for. They come from the Intendencia de
Montevideo's record of its public exercise classes for older people, shot by Santiago
Mazzarovich and Artigas Pessio between 2022 and 2025: a municipal programme photographed
honestly, people in their sixties, seventies and eighties in ordinary clothes. Nothing in
it is a model in a studio.

**The exercise tiles are instructions.** A person alone in a living room, three metres
from the television, has to work out what to do with their body from that tile, and a
candid photograph of a class cannot carry that however warm it is. So where published
instructional material exists for a movement, the tile uses it: BruceBlaus's home-care
exercise illustrations for Blausen Medical, which are real clinical teaching material
rather than something we drew. They are tone-mapped toward the app's warm palette and
mounted on their own ground; nothing else about them is changed.

Where no instructional image of a movement exists on Commons, the tile falls back to the
clearest photograph available, and the gaps are named below rather than hidden.

## Where Commons let us down

Three movements have no image on Commons in which the movement itself is legible, and
these tiles are the weakest in the app. Saying so is more useful than pretending:

- **Seated marching** — nothing shows a seated knee lift. The tile currently carries a
  photograph of a seated class, which shows the posture and the chair but not the lift.
- **Seated side reach** — nothing shows a seated lateral trunk bend.
- **Heel-to-toe stand** — the one candidate, a tandem walk along a balance beam, teaches
  the wrong setup for an app whose cue is "stand behind the chair". The tile carries a
  photograph of somebody mid-stride instead.

Searched, with nothing usable found: physical therapy, stretching, calisthenics, chair
exercise, range of motion exercise, gait training, Sitzgymnastik, gimnasia en silla,
tandem gait, Romberg test, lateral trunk flexion.

## The drawings that are still here

`art_feeling_too_easy`, `art_feeling_just_right` and `art_feeling_too_much` are vector
drawings, and stay that way on purpose. They are not pictures of anything: they are a
gauge, a line at the level the session was pitched at and a bar showing where it landed.
No photograph can say "that was harder than it should have been". They are built from
the shared SVGs in the hand-drawn source SVGs kept outside this app's own tree by `tools/svg-to-vector.py`.
